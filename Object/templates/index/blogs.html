AN ENTIRE WORLD OF LEARNING.

How does a machine tell a cat from a car? It starts with pixels, examples, and a pattern worth noticing.

01 / A world in ten categories.

CIFAR-10 is a collection of 60,000 color images, each just 32 × 32 pixels. It contains 50,000 training images and 10,000 test images, split across ten categories.

AirplaneAutomobileBirdCatDeerDogFrogHorseShipTruck

This small format makes the task surprisingly challenging. Fine details disappear, and the network has to learn which visual patterns matter.

02 / Learning to notice.

A convolutional neural network, or CNN, learns patterns in images. Its early layers respond to simple features such as edges and colors. Deeper layers combine those features into more complex shapes that help distinguish one category from another.

03 / The journey of an image.

  1. Prepare the picture.

    Your image is converted to RGB and resized to 32 × 32 pixels.

  2. Find the patterns.

    Convolutional filters look for useful features. Activations add flexibility, while pooling reduces the size of feature maps.

  3. Connect the clues.

    Deeper layers combine features into a representation used for classification.

  4. Explore the possibilities.

    The output assigns scores to the ten classes. PicScan shows the four highest-scoring predictions.

04 / Two ways of seeing.

PicScan compares a custom CNN with a pretrained ResNet-56. ResNet uses residual connections to help information move through a deeper network. Both models classify the image, and the result page shows their leading predictions.

The saved prediction comes from the model with the higher top confidence. Confidence scores from different models are not necessarily calibrated equally, so a higher score does not prove that one model is more accurate.

05 / Stay curious. Stay critical.

These models choose among ten known classes. An image of something outside those categories can still receive a confident prediction. Small images, unusual lighting, busy backgrounds, and similar-looking subjects can all lead to errors.

Use the results as an invitation to explore how machine learning works, and compare them with what you see yourself.
